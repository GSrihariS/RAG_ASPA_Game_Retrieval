There are far too many commands, awkward key combinations, and inconsistent interactions, while some basic functions aren't nearly as intuitive as they should be.

What's especially frustrating is that this is **Red Dead Redemption 2**—one of the most ambitious and polished games ever made. You would expect the control system to receive the same level of refinement as the graphics, world design, animation, and storytelling. Instead, the controls are one of the weakest parts of the entire experience.

A complicated game doesn't need complicated controls. Good controls should disappear into the background and let the player think about what they're doing. RDR2 too often makes the player think about **how to make the character do it**.

It's a shame because underneath the frustrating control system is an absolutely phenomenal game. I just wish Rockstar had spent as much time engineering the player experience as they did engineering the world.

**One of the worst control schemes I've encountered in a modern game**

Red Dead Redemption 2 is an incredible-looking game with an amazing world, story, characters, and attention to detail—but the controls are unnecessarily complicated, unintuitive, and at times downright frustrating.

The biggest problem is that the game seems to have been designed around an enormous number of context-sensitive actions rather than intuitive controls. Your character constantly has different actions mapped to the same buttons depending on what you're looking at, where you're standing, what you're holding, or what the game thinks you should be doing. Simple things like walking through a door, getting on a horse, picking something up, talking to someone, looting a body, or interacting with an object can become an exercise in figuring out which button the game wants you to press this time.
